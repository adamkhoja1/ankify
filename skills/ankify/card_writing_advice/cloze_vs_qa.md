# Cloze Deletions vs Question-Answer Pairs

Source: https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zPJt42JTcoAPTTTa2vdDonV
Author: Andy Matuschak

---

## Core Argument

Matuschak contends that while cloze deletions are easier to create, they produce shallower understanding than question-answer prompts in spaced repetition systems:

> "It's much easier to write Cloze deletion prompts: you can just copy/paste some source text and delete some phrase. But I find that prompts created this way tend to result in shallower understanding."

## Key Problems with Cloze Deletions

### Pattern Matching Over Integration

After repetitions, learners remember answers through pattern-matching rather than genuine conceptual integration—the surrounding text provides hints that enable surface-level recall.

### Structural Limitations

- They capture ideas from only one angle, whereas effective prompts encode multiple perspectives
- They lack atomic focus, typically containing narrative prose rather than concentrated concepts
- They require less intellectual effort to create, which itself undermines retention benefits
- Excessive wordiness includes irrelevant information

### Ambiguity Issues

Cloze deletions rely on syntactical structure to suggest answers, creating ambiguity about what response shape is expected.

### Answer Leakage

Non-deleted portions often inadvertently reveal the answer, undermining genuine memory retrieval.

## The Pragmatic Trade-off

Matuschak acknowledges cloze deletions' efficiency value:

> "The limiting factor in spaced repetition system capacity is writing enough good prompts."

Cloze deletions represent a reasonable compromise—they're vastly superior to creating nothing.

## When Cloze Works Well

- Memorizing verbatim texts (poems, speeches, formulas)
- Quick capture when time is limited
- Overlapping cloze deletions for sequences (testing one line at a time in context)
- Situations where engagement matters more than deep understanding

## Recommendation

Prefer question-answer pairs for conceptual understanding. Use cloze deletions pragmatically for:
- Verbatim memorization
- Quick capture with intent to refine later
- High-volume situations where some cards are better than none
